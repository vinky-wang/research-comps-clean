OBSERVATION-NOISE PRIOR SENSITIVITY — COMPLETED 2026-10-22

The PC priors substantially improve initialization repeatability for the
independent-clustering data and pass every numerical screen for Melding.
They do not fully stabilize PSmelding's parameter summaries, and they materially
change latent predictions. The proposed criterion of improved stability with
little predictive change is therefore not met generally. Keep the full study
on hold while investigating PSmelding's remaining integration/curvature sensitivity.

All 36 planned fits and scoring operations completed. Every attempt is retained.
No full simulation study was started and no production R script or original fit
was changed.

DESIGN AND CONTROLS
Two existing datasets, baseline gamma=1 and independent clustering delta=2,
with the same response field and true point/areal variances 0.1.
D=[0,1]^2, I=100, J=25, N=2500, rho=.2, sigma2=1, nu=1.
Both Melding and PSmelding were fitted under each prior at three initial areal
variances: .001, .1, .4. Initial point variance was .1 throughout.
Other starting values were held fixed within each dataset/model combination.

A: loggamma c(1, .00005)
B: pc.prec c(.316, .5)
C: pc.prec c(.5, .5)

Each prior was applied independently to BOTH observation precisions, which
remained separately estimated. A uses the original noise-prior parameters
inside this separate-precision extension; it is not an exact reconstruction
of the original repository's shared-precision model.

The saved finer mesh has 2832 vertices. Data, projectors, point-process
quadrature, spatial/fixed/copy priors and model formulas were retained.
Hyperparameter tolerance was 1e-4; other approximation/integration settings
were unchanged, with one INLA/BLAS/OpenMP thread and matched seeds.
This isolates noise-prior sensitivity under those numerical settings. It does
not assess the production mesh or every possible initialization direction.

WHY THE PRIORS DIFFER SUBSTANTIALLY
INLA pc.prec specifies P(sigma>u)=alpha, with sigma=1/sqrt(q).
B has median variance .316^2=.099856; C has median variance .25.
Prior P(variance<.001) is .951229 for A, .067014 for B, and .042891 for C.
Thus A strongly favors variances near zero on this scale. B/C still shrink
toward zero, but place far less probability in this near-zero interval.
Their effect is substantive prior regularization, not merely an optimizer fix.
Official calibration: https://inla.r-inla-download.org/r-inla.org/doc/prior/pc.prec.pdf
See prior_reference/ for analytic and installed-INLA checks.

NUMERICAL STABILITY
All 36 fits reported mode_status=0 and no negative Hessian eigenvalues.
All scored successfully; all requested parameter summaries passed the saved
validity checks, with no coherence flags. These status checks alone did not
identify the initialization sensitivity.

B/C Melding passed every prospective screen in BOTH datasets: 4 of 4 groups.
Every PSmelding group failed the parameter-repeatability screen. This screen
requires means, medians and 95% interval endpoints to differ by no more than
.1 pooled posterior SD between starts. Other screens and absolute differences
are saved separately; these practical thresholds are not convergence proofs.

Maximum parameter shifts under PC priors, in pooled posterior SD units:

For clustering, the improvement in mode and prediction repeatability is large:
A's log-mode spread is 3.477 for Melding and 21.381 for PSmelding.
Under B/C it is below .00005 for Melding and .00045 for PSmelding.
Maximum between-start RMS differences in posterior prediction means fall from
.301 (Melding) and .576 (PSmelding) under A to at most .000592 and .00851
under B/C. Nevertheless, PSmelding's intervals remain sensitive.

Baseline A Melding narrowly fails the parameter screen (.136 pooled SD at an
interval endpoint), while its means and predictions are quite repeatable.
Do not equate this small failure with the severe clustering instability under A.
Raw log posterior modes were compared only WITHIN a prior/model/dataset,
never across different priors.

  gamma 1.863 -> 1.893

  range .2809 -> .2871

The clustering comparison changes substantially if A's .001 start is used:
that solution has gamma 1.116, point variance .1576 and field variance 1.171.
This illustrates why a single A-versus-PC parameter contrast is misleading.
PSmelding omits the generating independent field U; gamma sensitivity here is
not evidence of recovery of a correctly specified sampling model.
Full parameter summaries and matched contrasts are retained. Nonlinear
microergodic summaries use transformed marginals, not transformed means.

LATENT PREDICTIVE PERFORMANCE
Scores target the retained latent Z(s), without future observation noise.
MSE uses posterior means; CRPS uses 1000 retained joint posterior draws.
Matched B/C-minus-A changes over all three starts:

Dataset / model              MSE relative change       CRPS absolute change
Baseline / Melding           -8.08% to -8.06%           -.01927 to -.01754
Baseline / PSmelding         -13.82% to -13.57%          -.03251 to -.02927
Clustering / Melding         -10.92% to +5.62%           -.02120 to +.00924
Clustering / PSmelding       +1.98% to +11.40%           +.00416 to +.03409

Negative changes mean better prediction. Baseline improvements exceed the
prospective 5% MSE and .01 absolute-CRPS materiality screens; they are not
negligible changes. Approximate matched CRPS Monte Carlo SEs are .00052–.00189,
well below the large changes. Small B-versus-C differences need more caution.

Clustering A has distinct numerical solutions, so its comparisons depend on
initialization. Do not average the starts as if they were independent simulation
replicates, or select the solution with the best score against the known truth.
B and C themselves give similar predictions: matched MSE differences are below
.81%, and the largest absolute CRPS difference is .00239, near Monte Carlo
uncertainty in some comparisons. This pilot does not establish a preferred prior
through predictive ranking.

NEXT STEP
Keep the full R=100 study on hold. Investigate PSmelding's remaining posterior
integration and Hessian-curvature sensitivity under one scientifically justified
PC candidate, checking interval endpoints as well as means and prediction RMS.
B is a reasonable candidate if a median observation SD of .316 is substantively
appropriate; retain C as sensitivity. Do not select a prior solely because it
scores best in these two saved datasets.

After numerical settings are stable, validate on additional independent
replicates before committing to the full study. These two datasets share one
underlying response-field realization, both Gaussian priors changed together,
and the finer mesh was held fixed. The pilot cannot establish general bias,
coverage, predictive superiority, or an isolated areal-prior effect.
